* Claim
For a positive integer $n$, the first $n$ integers satisfy the identity

$ sum_(k=1)^n k = (n(n+1))/2 $

* Proof
Pair the first and last terms. Each pair has sum $n+1$, so adding the
sequence to its reversal gives

$ 2 sum_(k=1)^n k = n(n+1) $

Dividing by two proves the claim.

* Check
For $n=4$, the sum is $1+2+3+4=10$, which agrees with $(4 dot 5)/2$.
